# Visual Identity Brief

## What this skill does

Gives designers what they actually need - positioning, audience, territories to explore and how the decision gets made - instead of 'make it modern'.

## Inputs to collect

- Positioning statement and messaging framework
- Audience and competitive visual landscape
- Applications list: where the identity must work (site, ads, decks, social, product)

## Workflow

1. **State the strategic job** - What the visuals must make true in the viewer's mind, tied to positioning. Include the current identity's equity: what must be kept, what may go.
2. **Map the visual landscape** - Competitor and category visual audit: colours, typography, imagery styles in use. Identify the crowded territory and the open territory.
3. **Define 2-3 territories** - Distinct creative directions to explore, each with a rationale and reference board (references as direction, never as copying targets).
4. **List deliverables by application** - Logo system, type, colour, imagery, templates for the real applications, and the mini-guideline format the team will actually use.
5. **Fix the decision process** - Who decides, on what criteria (strategy fit, distinctiveness, usability at small sizes, accessibility contrast), and how many rounds. Prevent design-by-committee before it starts.

## Deliverable

- Visual identity brief with territories
- Competitor visual landscape map
- Deliverables and decision-process sheet

## Success metrics

| Metric | How to read it |
|--------|---------------|
| Distinctiveness test | Unaided: can viewers pick the brand's asset from a category lineup? |
| Application coverage | Share of real use cases with a working template at launch |
| Accessibility compliance | Contrast ratios pass for text-bearing assets |

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Territories genuinely distinct, not one idea in three colours
- [ ] Decision criteria agreed before work is shown
- [ ] Small-size and dark-mode usability explicitly required

## Guardrails

- Claims discipline: use only proof the user supplies or verifies. Never invent clients, results, rankings, or testimonials.
- Privacy and consent: collect and use personal data only with a lawful basis and consent where required. Respect PDPA (Singapore) and GDPR; honour unsubscribes and opt-outs immediately.
- Platform policy: follow each ad and search platform's current policies. When a rule changes often, flag it for a human to re-check before launch.
- Brand claims obey the same proof rules as performance claims. Aspiration is fine; fabrication is not.
- Reference boards guide direction; never brief a designer to imitate a specific brand's protected trade dress.
