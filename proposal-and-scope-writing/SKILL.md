# Proposal and Scope Writing

## What this skill does

A proposal format that does two jobs: makes the client confident to sign, and makes the engagement deliverable without scope-creep warfare.

## Inputs to collect

- Discovery notes and agreed goals
- Service delivery capacity and real timelines
- Pricing model (retainer, project, hybrid) and margin floors

## Workflow

1. **Open with their problem and outcomes** - Restate the client's situation and the outcomes this engagement targets - in their words from discovery. Services come after outcomes, always.
2. **Scope in deliverables and cadence** - What gets produced, how often, to what standard, with whose input. Replace effort language ('support', 'management') with countable deliverables and named rituals.
3. **Write the exclusions explicitly** - What is not included (ad spend, tools, development, copywriting volumes beyond X) and how extras get priced. Exclusions written kindly now prevent arguments later.
4. **Set timeline and dependencies** - Milestones with dates, and the client-side dependencies beside each (access, approvals, assets) with the consequence of delays stated neutrally.
5. **Include change control and success measures** - How changes are requested, estimated and approved; and the metrics this engagement will be reported against - the same ones from the baseline.

## Deliverable

- Proposal: outcomes, scope, exclusions, timeline, investment
- Change-control clause in plain language
- Success measures tied to the baseline

## Success metrics

| Metric | How to read it |
|--------|---------------|
| Proposal win rate | By service line - informs both pricing and qualification |
| Scope-creep incidents per engagement | Unpaid out-of-scope work episodes; should fall as scopes sharpen |
| Delivery margin vs planned | Proposals priced on fantasy timelines show up here |

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every deliverable is countable or observable
- [ ] Exclusions written as clearly as inclusions
- [ ] Success metrics match the discovery baseline

## Guardrails

- Claims discipline: use only proof the user supplies or verifies. Never invent clients, results, rankings, or testimonials.
- Privacy and consent: collect and use personal data only with a lawful basis and consent where required. Respect PDPA (Singapore) and GDPR; honour unsubscribes and opt-outs immediately.
- Platform policy: follow each ad and search platform's current policies. When a rule changes often, flag it for a human to re-check before launch.
- Client data stays confidential and is used only for that client's work. Never reuse one client's data, results or creative for another client's pitch without written permission.
- Report client results honestly, including the misses. A client who discovers a hidden failure leaves; a client told early usually stays.
