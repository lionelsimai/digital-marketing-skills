# Brand Positioning Workshop

## What this skill does

A facilitated session (plus pre-work) that produces a usable positioning statement and - harder - agreement on what the brand is not.

## Inputs to collect

- Customer research and persona evidence
- Competitor positioning audit
- Leadership's ambitions and constraints for the brand

## Workflow

1. **Do the pre-work** - Participants read the customer evidence and competitor audit before the room. Workshops that start from opinions end with opinions.
2. **Fix the audience and frame** - One primary audience, one frame of reference (what category the buyer puts you in). Both are choices with consequences - record the alternatives rejected.
3. **Fight for the differentiator** - List candidate differentiators; subject each to the test: does the audience care, can rivals copy it in a quarter, can we prove it today? Survivors only.
4. **Attach reasons to believe** - Each claim gets its proof from the claims register. Unprovable claims are parked as aspirations with an owner and a plan to earn them.
5. **Write it and stress-test it** - Positioning statement in plain words, then tested against a homepage headline, a sales opening and a hiring pitch. If it only works in the workshop doc, it doesn't work.

## Deliverable

- Positioning statement with rejected alternatives logged
- Reasons-to-believe map tied to the claims register
- Application tests: headline, sales opener, pitch line

## Success metrics

| Metric | How to read it |
|--------|---------------|
| Message consistency across surfaces | Audit home, ads, decks quarterly against the statement |
| Sales adoption | Reps using the positioning unprompted in calls (sampled) |
| Win/loss mentions of positioning themes | From deal debriefs over two quarters |

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Decisions based on customer evidence presented in pre-work
- [ ] At least one explicit 'we are not' recorded
- [ ] Every differentiator has proof available today

## Guardrails

- Claims discipline: use only proof the user supplies or verifies. Never invent clients, results, rankings, or testimonials.
- Privacy and consent: collect and use personal data only with a lawful basis and consent where required. Respect PDPA (Singapore) and GDPR; honour unsubscribes and opt-outs immediately.
- Platform policy: follow each ad and search platform's current policies. When a rule changes often, flag it for a human to re-check before launch.
- Brand claims obey the same proof rules as performance claims. Aspiration is fine; fabrication is not.
