# ICE / RICE Prioritization

## What this skill does

Makes prioritisation a repeatable, arguable-in-a-good-way exercise: scores are explicit, confidence is discounted by evidence, and past estimate accuracy feeds back in.

## Inputs to collect

- Experiment backlog with hypotheses
- Baseline metrics each experiment targets
- Historical results: predicted vs actual for past experiments

## Workflow

1. **Choose the framework** - ICE (Impact, Confidence, Effort) for speed; RICE (adds Reach) when audience sizes differ wildly. One framework per programme - mixing scores makes ranks meaningless.
2. **Anchor the scales** - Define what a 5 and a 10 mean in this business (Impact 10 = moves the primary metric 25%+). Anchors stop every idea being scored 8/10.
3. **Score confidence by evidence type** - Direct data from your funnel > user research > competitor observation > opinion. Write the mapping down; confidence is where optimism hides.
4. **Calibrate with history** - Compare last cycle's predicted impacts with actuals and apply the team's optimism factor to new estimates. If you historically over-predict 2x, halve the impacts.
5. **Sequence with constraints** - Top scores get scheduled, respecting dependencies and the rule that a surface runs one major test at a time. Publish the ranked list and the reasoning.

## Deliverable

- Scored, ranked experiment list with anchors documented
- Calibration note (optimism factor and its source)
- Cycle schedule respecting surface constraints

## Success metrics

| Metric | How to read it |
|--------|---------------|
| Estimate accuracy trend | Predicted vs actual effect across cycles - should converge |
| Top-5 completion rate | Prioritisation is real only if the top gets run |
| Score-to-outcome correlation | Do high scores actually win more often? Check quarterly |

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Scales anchored in this business's numbers
- [ ] Confidence tied to evidence type by a written mapping
- [ ] Ranked list published to the team with reasoning

## Guardrails

- Claims discipline: use only proof the user supplies or verifies. Never invent clients, results, rankings, or testimonials.
- Privacy and consent: collect and use personal data only with a lawful basis and consent where required. Respect PDPA (Singapore) and GDPR; honour unsubscribes and opt-outs immediately.
- Platform policy: follow each ad and search platform's current policies. When a rule changes often, flag it for a human to re-check before launch.
- Growth never overrides consent or honesty: no spam invites, no contact scraping for referral mechanics, no dark-pattern virality.
- Experiments on pricing and billing must be transparent to affected users - no silent price tests on existing customers' renewals.
