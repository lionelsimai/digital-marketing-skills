# Form Optimization

## What this skill does

Treats every field as a price the visitor pays in effort, and makes sure the business only charges for data it actually uses.

## Inputs to collect

- Current form(s) with completion analytics by field if available
- List of fields and who uses each one downstream (honestly)
- Qualification requirements from sales/ops

## Workflow

1. **Audit every field's right to exist** - For each field: who uses this, for what decision, what happens if it's empty? Fields without answers get removed or made optional.
2. **Order by ease and logic** - Easy, non-sensitive fields first (name, email); commitment-heavy fields (phone, budget, company size) later, grouped logically. Ask for the hardest thing at the moment of highest motivation.
3. **Design help and errors kindly** - Inline explanations where format matters, errors that say what to fix in human words, preserved input on error (never clear the form), and mobile-friendly inputs (right keyboards, autofill support).
4. **Consider multi-step honestly** - Multi-step helps when questions branch or the ask is large; show progress truthfully. Never use multi-step to hide the total ask until it's too late to leave.
5. **Close the loop after submit** - Confirmation that states what happens next and when, plus the delivery email. The form's job ends at a kept promise, not a spinner.

## Deliverable

- Field-by-field audit with keep/optional/remove verdicts
- Redesigned form spec: order, help text, error messages
- Post-submit confirmation copy and flow

## Success metrics

| Metric | How to read it |
|--------|---------------|
| Form start-to-complete rate | The headline number |
| Abandonment by field | Pinpoints the exact price visitors refuse to pay |
| Lead quality of completions | Completion up + quality down is a targeting problem, not a win |

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every remaining field has a named downstream user
- [ ] Error states tested with real invalid input
- [ ] Input preserved on validation errors

## Guardrails

- Claims discipline: use only proof the user supplies or verifies. Never invent clients, results, rankings, or testimonials.
- Privacy and consent: collect and use personal data only with a lawful basis and consent where required. Respect PDPA (Singapore) and GDPR; honour unsubscribes and opt-outs immediately.
- Platform policy: follow each ad and search platform's current policies. When a rule changes often, flag it for a human to re-check before launch.
- No dark patterns: no fake countdowns, confirm-shaming, hidden costs or pre-ticked add-ons. Conversion bought with deception is refunded with interest in trust.
- Social proof must be real: genuine testimonials with permission, accurate numbers, no fabricated reviews or logos.
- Never pre-tick consent or marketing opt-ins inside a form, and never make a required field out of something the visitor can't know.
